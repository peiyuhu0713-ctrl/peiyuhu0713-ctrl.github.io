Experiment Platform Interface Flow

Experiment Platform Interface Flow

Participant-facing wireframes for the study of default visual representations and user-side repair cost. Each screen states what is displayed, which controls are enabled at that stage, and what the system records there.

Enabled here Visible but disabled Recorded

24 screens end to end

01 Welcome and consent→ 02 Pre-test→ Assignment (no screen)→ 03 Creative brief→ 04 Initial image and questions→ 05 Intention→ 06 Repair→ 07 Task complete × 4 tasks → 23 Post-test→ 24 Debrief

Tasks 2, 3 and 4 are screen-for-screen identical to Task 1; only the stimulus drawn differs. The five screens of one task are drawn in full below; the rest are not repeated.

Mandated repair stage: Submit is disabled. It unlocks only after the prompt has been changed at least once and one new image has been generated successfully.

Generation history (visible to the participant)

Shows only the participant's own generations; no wording suggestions and no prompt recommendations

Recorded at every Generate

  • The full prompt submitted with this request, stored as complete text, never as a diff
  • When Generate was selected
  • When the image was returned
  • Generation attempt count incremented; success and failure counted separately
    generation_attempt_count · generation_success_count · generation_failure_count
  • The resulting image, stored and identified
  • Model version, generation parameters, seed

Timing

  • Timing starts on entering the repair screen
  • The wait between Generate and the image returning must be excluded; the participant is not working during it
  • Tab-away and idle intervals are recorded separately

Condition for completing the mandated repair

  • The prompt differs from the initial version and at least one new image has been generated successfully. Submit unlocks only when both hold

How continued repair is determined

  • After the mandated generation, generations the participant issuesof their own accord— the second and any further ones — are recorded as continued repair
  • The system must not prompt or encourage further revision in any way

Screen for screen identical to Task 1: creative brief → initial image and questions → intention → repair → task complete. The only differences are the task content and the stimulus drawn. Q1 When a prompt does not specify a person's gender, what may happen in a text-to-image model's output?

○ Certain occupations or scenes skew systematically towards one gender
○ Gender is determined entirely by the random seed
○ The model does not generate people
○ Not sure

Q2 When a prompt does not specify a country or region, what may characterise the scenes a model generates?

○ An even spread across the world
○ Concentration on the visual environment of a few countries or regions
○ No regional features at all
○ Determined entirely by the random seed
○ Not sure

Q3 What may influence the visual representations that recur in a text-to-image model?

○ Training data alone
○ Prompt wording alone
○ Training data, model design, prompt wording and generation settings acting together
○ Entirely random, with no systematic source
○ Not sure

Screen count: two lead-in screens (01–02), five screens per task making twenty, plus post-test and debrief, giving twenty-four in all.

These wireframes specify interface structure, control states and the points at which data are recorded. They do not specify visual styling. Question wording, scale anchors and button labels are taken from the study configuration.

The platform does not compute the BRBI, the four perception–intention states, final compliance, or the actual repair strategy; these are coded by the researchers afterwards. The platform is responsible only for recording and exporting the raw data in full and in a linkable form.
